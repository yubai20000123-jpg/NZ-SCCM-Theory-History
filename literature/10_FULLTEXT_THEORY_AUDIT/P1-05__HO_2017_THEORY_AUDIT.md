# P1-05 — Ho, Doh & Fragomeni (2017) theory audit

Evidence scope: Griffith Research Online accepted manuscript, 33 pages. The repository rights statement identifies it as the peer-reviewed accepted version and permits non-commercial self-archiving under Wiley terms.

## A. STRUCTURAL_OBJECT

- Object: reinforced-concrete wall panels in one-way (OW), three-side (TW3S) and four-side (TW4S) support conditions.
- Loading: axial compression with eccentricity; wall height, width, thickness, concrete strength, reinforcement ratio and support restraints vary.
- Imperfection: assumed sinusoidal initial/midspan deflection; side restraints are represented by horizontal beam strips in two-way action.
- Material: concrete compression models (Saenz for NSC; Lu–Zhao for HSC), concrete tension/tension-stiffening (Fields–Bischoff), elastic-perfectly-plastic reinforcement.

## B. KINEMATICS

- OW strip: `Y_i = Y_0 sin[pi(i/2n)]`; beam-column unit strip, plane sections remain plane.
- TW wall: `Y_ij = Y_mn sin(pi i/2m) sin(pi j/2n)` (Eq. 10); the assumed mode satisfies the selected support symmetry.
- Classification: `THEORETICAL_MODE + FINITE_DIFFERENCE/NUMERICAL_INTEGRATION`; not Airy/Galerkin plate theory.

## C. IN_PLANE_EQUILIBRIUM

- No Airy stress function. Axial force is imposed on strips and section resultants are obtained by fibre integration.
- Internal beam resistance is transferred to the column strips through grid-point forces `F_ij` (Eq. 13); this is a strip-equilibrium replacement for a 2D membrane field.

## D. COMPATIBILITY

- Section compatibility: plane sections before bending remain plane after bending; a linear strain profile is imposed through the section.
- Wall compatibility: finite-difference curvature and numerical double integration update deflection/slope.
- Classification: `EXACT_SECTION_COMPATIBILITY + APPROXIMATE_GLOBAL_MODE + ITERATIVE_GRID_COMPATIBILITY`.

## E. TRANSVERSE_EQUILIBRIUM

- OW: beam-column `M_i = N Y_i + M_max`, with curvature read from the section `M-kappa` curve and Newmark double integration (Eq. 9).
- TW: beam-strip curvature (Eq. 11–12), internal resistance `F_ij` (Eq. 13), column moment equilibrium (Eq. 14), and numerical integration (Eq. 15).
- This is a nonlinear beam-column/strip equilibrium model, not Marguerre–von Kármán/Airy plate equilibrium.

## F. POSTBUCKLING_RELATION

- `NO_EXPLICIT_POSTBUCKLING_RELATION` of the form `P=P(q)` is given.
- The response is generated by incrementing initial midspan deflection and iterating grid spacing/curvature until convergence; failure occurs when a section moment reaches ultimate moment capacity or the height–deflection curve peaks.
- `Pcr` is an output of the instability search, not a closed symbolic parameter.

## G. STRUCTURAL_DEMAND

- Section demand: `N` and `M` from fibre integration; wall demand: `M_i`, `F_ij`, deflection `Y_ij` and curvature `kappa_ij`.
- Critical location is the strip/grid point where moment first exceeds ultimate section moment or where the height–deflection curve reaches its maximum.
- No effective width is used as the principal operator; code equations are included as empirical comparators.

## CAPACITY_LAYER

- Section capacity comes from material stress–strain laws and fibre sums:
  - `N = sum(A_i sigma_i) + sum(A_si sigma_si)` (Eq. 3-1)
  - `M = sum(A_i y_i sigma_i) + sum(A_si y_si sigma_si)` (Eq. 3-2)
- Ultimate section capacity is the peak of the `M-kappa` curve. Concrete cracking, tension stiffening, compression softening and steel yielding are material/section capacity ingredients.

## CONTROL_LOCATION_SOURCE

`INCREMENTAL_SEARCH + ASSUMED + TEST_INFORMED` — the sine mode and strip layout are assumed; the governing grid point/peak is found during the iterative analysis and checked against tests.

## ULTIMATE_SOLVER

`INCREMENTAL_LOAD_PATH + PARAMETRIC_SEARCH + MATERIAL_HISTORY`.

- Requires load/deflection increments, section stress–strain iteration, spatial strip/grid points and convergence tolerance.
- Does not use Newton/arc-length in this 2017 WASTABT paper; it uses repeated curvature/deflection integration and a peak/limit check.

## EMPIRICISM_LOCATION

`MATERIAL_ONLY + CONTROL_LOCATION + WHOLE_PANEL_STRENGTH` — constitutive laws and code comparators are empirical/semi-empirical; the sine shape and effective-height logic simplify structural response; predicted failure loads are calibrated/checked against tests.

## VERIFIED_CLASS

`CLASS_CD_VERIFIED` — mechanics-based section/strip demand is present, but the ultimate closure is incremental and the material/control assumptions are empirical or test-informed.

## LESSONS_FOR_NZ_SCCM

1. Mature step to adopt: fibre-section `N-M` capacity integration and explicit separation of section capacity from global instability demand.
2. Useful structural pattern: use a section capacity module as the constitutive interface while keeping global equilibrium independent.
3. Closed form is lost because cracking, tension stiffening, descending compression and support interaction make `M-kappa` path-dependent/nonlinear.
4. Generality is lost through assumed sine modes, strip discretisation and support-specific WASTABT algorithms.
5. Empirical correction is needed because concrete constitutive response, cracking and effective-height/support restraint cannot be represented by a single closed expression.
6. Incremental method is needed to update curvature, cracking, material stiffness and grid equilibrium.
7. The first complexity source is the section `M-kappa` relation, before the 2D wall grid.
8. A steel–UHPC migration first fails at the heterogeneous section capacity and connector/interface law.
9. NZ-SCCM may avoid unnecessary global stepping if a path-independent UHPC/steel capacity surface is acceptable for the target limit state.
10. NZ-SCCM should not replicate WASTABT's strip iteration when a finite direct capacity intersection can represent the required failure mode.
