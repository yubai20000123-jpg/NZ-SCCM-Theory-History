# P0-02 — Isami (1994) theory audit

Evidence scope: J-STAGE PDF, 9 pages. Text extraction preserves equation numbering but some symbols are corrupted; quoted forms below follow the readable equation structure and the paper's explicit prose.

## A. STRUCTURAL_OBJECT

- Object: rectangular stiffened steel plate with `ns` longitudinal stiffeners.
- Boundary/loading: all four edges simply supported; biaxial in-plane forces/stresses `sigma_x, sigma_y`, including tension/compression combinations.
- Imperfection: residual stress distributions and initial out-of-plane imperfection `W0` are prescribed; the actual imperfection is mapped to an equivalent amplitude.
- Material: elastic-perfectly-plastic plate and stiffener; secant/tangent moduli are updated algebraically in the elasto-plastic range.

## B. KINEMATICS

- Eq. (8) defines `W` and `W0` with modal half-wave numbers `m,n`; all out-of-plane amplitudes are normalised by plate thickness.
- The same modal family covers local modes `L1–L5` and global modes `G1–G2`.
- Classification: `THEORETICAL_MODE + GALERKIN`; not FE-derived.

## C. IN_PLANE_EQUILIBRIUM

- The paper explicitly uses a **modified Airy stress function** `F` (p. 24s, Eq. 9–10).
- Standard resultant mapping is used in the modified orthotropic plate formulation: `Nx = F,yy`, `Ny = F,xx`, `Nxy = -F,xy` up to the paper's sign convention and normalisation.
- Orthotropy is introduced through equivalent thickness/rigidity coefficients `k1–k4`; the secant modulus `Es` represents the current elasto-plastic stiffness.

## D. COMPATIBILITY

- Eq. (9) is the nonlinear compatibility/equilibrium system for the orthotropic perfect plate; Eq. (10) defines the biharmonic operator and transverse operator.
- The Galerkin projection of the modal fields produces the algebraic post-buckling relation.
- Classification: `GALERKIN + APPROXIMATE` after an analytically defined Airy/Kármán field.

## E. TRANSVERSE_EQUILIBRIUM

- The governing equations are a modified von Kármán/Marguerre-type large-deflection orthotropic-plate system (Eq. 9–10).
- The transverse operator contains the orthotropic bending/torsional rigidity constants and the nonlinear membrane coupling generated by `F`.

## F. POSTBUCKLING_RELATION

- For the perfect plate, the paper gives the explicit quadratic relation (Eq. 13): `sigma_eq = sigma_eq,cr + C_p W^2`.
- `W` is the modal out-of-plane amplitude; `W0` is the initial imperfection amplitude; `sigma_eq,cr` is the equivalent bifurcation stress.
- The stress ratio `p = sigma_y/sigma_x` is evaluated at buckling and then held fixed along the post-buckling branch (Eq. 14–18).
- The plastic failure-mechanism curve is given by Eq. (23–25). Ultimate strength is the intersection of Eq. (13) and Eq. (24), obtained from the **cubic polynomial Eq. (26)**. The paper therefore supplies a finite direct root closure.
- The imperfect-plate ultimate strength is then obtained from the equivalent bifurcation point, the slope of the mechanism curve (Eq. 27–29), and the maximum on the failure-mechanism curve.

## G. STRUCTURAL_DEMAND

- Demand variables: equivalent membrane stress, `Nx, Ny, Nxy` through `F`, and bending moment on the plastic fold line `M/Mp`.
- Candidate critical locations/modes: five local plate modes `L1–L5` and two global stiffened-plate modes `G1–G2`.
- No effective width or empirical whole-panel strength factor appears in the primary closure. Residual stresses, secant modulus, and equivalent imperfection are assumed inputs/operators.

## CAPACITY_LAYER

- Eq. (23) is a combined membrane-stress/fold-moment interaction condition, with `M_p` the full plastic moment.
- Capacity is a plastic mechanism surface, not a single pointwise `sigma=f_y` check.
- Concrete/UHPC capacity: not applicable.

## CONTROL_LOCATION_SOURCE

`FINITE_BOUNDARY_CANDIDATES + ASSUMED` — the seven modes are an analytically enumerated candidate set; the controlling mode is the governing intersection/lowest ultimate interaction curve.

## ULTIMATE_SOLVER

`FINITE_POLYNOMIAL_ROOT + DIRECT_DEMAND_CAPACITY_INTERSECTION`. No load stepping, Newton path, arc-length continuation, or spatial material-history integration is required.

## EMPIRICISM_LOCATION

`MULTIPLE`: prescribed residual-stress profiles, fixed post-buckling stress ratio, equivalent-imperfection mapping, and selected mode catalogue. These are structural idealisations, but not an empirical whole-panel `Pu` reduction.

## VERIFIED_CLASS

`CLASS_B_VERIFIED` — the acquired PDF explicitly demonstrates mechanics-derived structural demand, a separate plastic-mechanism capacity curve, and cubic finite-root ultimate closure.

## LESSONS_FOR_NZ_SCCM

1. The mature mathematical chain is already clear: Airy/Kármán field → Galerkin projection → quadratic post-buckling curve → mechanism curve → cubic root.
2. The cubic intersection architecture is directly reusable, but the capacity curve must be replaced for heterogeneous steel–concrete/UHPC sections.
3. Closed form is obtained by fixing a modal family, stress ratio and mechanism catalogue.
4. Generality is lost through fixed stress ratio, prescribed residual stresses, equivalent imperfection and seven preselected modes.
5. The paper does not need empirical strength reduction because the structural demand and mechanism capacity are both explicit; its empirical content is pushed into inputs/idealisation.
6. Incremental methods are avoided because the material is elastic-perfectly-plastic and the final criterion is path-independent.
7. Complexity first appears in mode competition and imperfection sensitivity.
8. Transfer to concrete/UHPC first fails at the membrane-plus-bending capacity surface and at tension/cracking/softening, not at Airy compatibility.
9. NZ-SCCM's replaceable capacity module is the correct way to retain the structural operator.
10. Adding a full Newton/arc-length history to the same path-independent capacity problem would be unnecessary unless cracking/softening history is explicitly required.
