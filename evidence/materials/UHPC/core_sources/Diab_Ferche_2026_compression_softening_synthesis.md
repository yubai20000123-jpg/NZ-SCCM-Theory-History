## Effective evidence retained

The paper synthesizes cracked-UHPC compression-softening data from multiple experimental programs, including Fehling, Lee, Leutbecher, Liu and other studies, and compares existing softening models with a proposed model.

The source writes the cracked-UHPC peak compressive strength as

```text
f_p = beta * f'_c
```

where `beta` is a compression-softening factor governed by transverse tensile/cracking response.

A source modeling choice is that `beta` is applied to compressive strength but not to the strain at peak compressive strength, citing experimental observations that the peak strain is similar between uniaxial compression and biaxial tension-compression in the referenced study.

The compiled evidence indicates:

- fibre reinforcement at about 1% already materially reduces the severity of compression softening relative to non-fibre UHPC;
- within the compiled roughly 1.0%–2.5% fibre range, no clear monotonic additional fibre-content trend was discerned;
- transverse tensile strain and the presence of fibre reinforcement are therefore identified as dominant variables in the compiled response.

Its comparison table/figure combines heterogeneous programs with different specimen dimensions, reinforcement ratios, fibre types and fibre contents. This makes the paper valuable as a **cross-source trend synthesis**, but not a substitute for the individual primary test programs when precise material identification is required.
