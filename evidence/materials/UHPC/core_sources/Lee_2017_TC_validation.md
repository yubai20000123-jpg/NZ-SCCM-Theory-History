## Effective evidence retained

The study tests 30 panel specimens under different transverse tensile strains using sequential tension-then-compression loading. The experimental program varies:

- applied tensile strain (up to about 0.003 in the reported program);
- fibre volume fraction (0, 1.0 or 1.5% depending on series);
- concrete mix/compressive strength;
- presence/absence of transverse bar reinforcement.

The reported material-level findings include:

- steel fibres materially increase tension-stiffening; the paper reports a tension-stiffening bond factor for fibre-reinforced UHPC roughly twice that of the non-fibre case in the studied configurations;
- under the largest transverse tensile loading levels, UHPFRC compressive strength decreases by roughly 10–30%, while UHPC without fibres decreases by roughly 30–50%;
- sequential loading is explicitly treated as a lower/conservative boundary for transverse-tension-induced compression reduction;
- the test set is dominated by compression failure in the TC quadrant and does not span the complete tension-failure region.

The paper also proposes schematic biaxial failure criteria, but those criteria combine the panel results with external references and assumptions needed to convert element response to material strength.

## USED FOR

- independent validation of TC compression-softening trends;
- checking the beneficial effect of steel fibres on the transverse-tension penalty;
- lower-bound/conservative sequential-loading evidence;
- material-level range check for a future TC interaction law.

## NOT USED FOR

- direct transfer of the reported 10–30% reduction to a different 2%-fibre UHPC without qualification;
- complete TT/CC/C3 constitutive closure;
- arbitrary path update law;
- exact current-operator coefficient fitting from structural ultimate loads.
