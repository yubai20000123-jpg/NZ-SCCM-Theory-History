3.1 INTRODUCTION



The load capacity of walls under in-plane loads depends on the strength of the

concrete, the amount and position of reinforcement, wall imperfections, the load

eccentricity and the wall slenderness ratio. Apart from these, the wall load capacity

is also related to the ratio of the width and height of the wall, the boundary

conditions of the wall sides, and the loading such as uniform compression,

compression with in-plane eccentricity or shear loading. The investigation of all the

factors affecting the wall load capacity, therefore, requires the study of many

parameters. The current Australian standard, AS 3600, restricts the wall by its

slenderness (Hwe I tw < 30, where tw is the wall thickness, and Hwe is the effective

height of the wall). Slenderness is therefore an important factor in wall design.

Attard (1994) showed that AS 3600 is very conservative for walls in two-way

bending. Since experimental work although essential is an expensive undertaking

especially with regard to the study of the buckling of walls, an analytical formulation

based on the finite element method will be developed in this thesis.



In this chapter, a brief literature review of practical wall design and the buckling of

concrete walls is reported. The constitutive models for concrete and reinforcement

under different states are also discussed here. These are important for the finite

element formulations developed in Chapter 4 and Chapter 6 for stability analysis of

reinforced concrete walls.

1989. The method involves a buckling analysis. Only the method for determining

the effective height of the wall is described here.

A detailed discussion and comparison of the wall design formulae according to AS

3600, ACI 318-1989 and DIN 1045 can be found in Fragomeni, Mend.is and

Grayson (1994) and Raviskanthan, Sanjayan and Al-Mahadi (1995). Fragomeni et

al. (1994) showed that the wall design practice in the United States involving the use

of the standard ACI 318-1989 is intended for load-bearing walls restrained at the

top and bottom edges. The code fails to recognise any contribution to load capacity

from restraint on the side edges. These boundary conditions are common for walls

in core walls. In AS 3600, the contribution to the load capacity from the side edges

is taken into account through the effective height of the wall. 'The wall effective

height in the AS 3600 design formu1a depends on the restraint condition of the

edges, and the wall height or the wall width whichever is critical. However, both

the formu1as in ACI 318-1989 and AS 3600 are based on one-way bending. In the

German Code, DIN 1045, the determination of the effective Jength of walls

restrained on two, three, and four sides depends on a reduction factor k . Fragomeni

Code effective Jength factor for walls supported on three or four sides, the increase
