6.3.1 Layered Elements



Hand et al (1973) used a layered element with 20 degrees of freedom to· analyse

reinforced concrete walls under out-of-plane bending. The stress-s~ relationship

of concrete was assumed to be bilinear elastic-plastic in compression and linear with

limited strength in tension, while the steel was considered to be an elastic-plastic

material. In each layer, the stress-strain relationship of concrete was assumed to be

unchanged over the layer thickness, and the failure criterion for plain concrete in

biaxial stress states proposed by Kupfer, Hilsdorf, and Rusch (1969) was used with

slight modification. The second-order effects of the in-plane stresses of the wall and

shells were not considered.




Wanchoo and May (1975) also used the layered element to model reinforced

concrete walls. They examined the out-of-plane bending behaviour of walls, and the

cracks caused by tension due to the bending of concrete were considered. The

concrete was modelled as being elastic-cracked in tension, and elastic-plastic in

compression.    The reinforcing steel was modelled by a two-dimensional layer

obeying the von Mises yieJd criterion and the associated flow law. Perfect shear

bond was assumed between layers and the transverse shearing deformation was

neglected. Like Hand et al (1973), the second-order effects of the in-plane stresses

were not considered.
