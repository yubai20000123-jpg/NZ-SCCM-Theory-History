6.1 INTRODUCTION



As for most structural elements, reinforced concrete walls used in civil engineering

always have initial out-of-plane imperfections due to the limitations of construction

technology. The eccentricity of the axial forces can occur either accidentally or

intentionally as when floor loads are transferred to a supporting wall (Figure 6.1).

The initial imperfections influence the magnitude of the second-order effects

associated with any applied axial forces on the out-of-plane bending moments.

Hence, initial imperfections and eccentricity must be considered in the design of

walls. In Building Codes such as AS 3600 and ACI 318-1989 as reviewed in

Chapter 3, the initial out-of-plane loading eccentricity and the effects of initial

imperfections are incorporated into the wall capacity design formula.



Reinforced concrete walls are different from columns because of the two-way nature

of the bending distribution.   To solve for the out-of-plane bending induced by the

presence of initial imperfections and out-of-plane loading eccentricities involves the

solution of the bending equations for plates under end applied axial force and

bending moment. Timoshenko and Woinowsky-Kreiger (1959) present a numerical

solution to the classical case for this problem and Attard (1995) presents an

approximate solution for reinforced concrete walls.        The finite element method

offers, however, an effective tool for investigating the effects of initial imperfections

and loading eccentricities on the capacity of reinforced concrete walls.
