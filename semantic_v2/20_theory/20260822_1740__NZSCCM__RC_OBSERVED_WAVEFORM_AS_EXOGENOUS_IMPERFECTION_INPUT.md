## 1. Role of waveform

The waveform is an exogenous structural input, analogous to a measured initial-imperfection field. Its coefficients/geometry may be recovered from experiment, published deflection curves, photographs, or FE reconstructions of the observed shape, but they are not selected using experimental failure load `Pf`.

The waveform may be nonstandard, asymmetric, unequal-lobed, or have an effective local scale between the integer Navier half-wave lengths. That is acceptable because the objective is diagnostic, not prediction of imperfection formation.

## 2. Preserve the explicit architecture

The current explicit calculation method remains the required backend. The single-sine formulas `Pcr,C,G,J` are not blindly reused for an arbitrary waveform; instead they must be regenerated as exact finite shape-function functionals of the prescribed analytic waveform.

A generic prescribed waveform can be written as

\[
w_0(x,y)=A_0\,X(x)\Phi_0(y),
\qquad
w(x,y)=A\,X(x)\Phi(y),
\]

with the observed/current shape represented by a finite analytic basis whenever needed, e.g.

\[
\Phi(y)=\sum_{r=1}^{R} c_r\sin\frac{r\pi y}{a}.
\]

Here `c_r` are **geometry inputs recovered from waveform evidence**, not structural unknowns chosen to minimize load error.

For a piecewise/local observed bulge, an equivalent finite piecewise-analytic representation is also admissible if continuity/boundary conditions are explicit and all required integrals are exact.

## 4. Diagnostic logic for the material model

For each specimen with a sufficiently recovered waveform:

1. freeze geometry/material input other than the waveform;
2. freeze the observed waveform representation independently of `Pf`;
3. regenerate the explicit structural demand generated by that waveform;
4. apply the same fixed 2D material constraint/current operator;
5. obtain predicted material limiting state and predicted capacity/event;
6. only after the theoretical event is frozen, compare with experimental `Pf` and observed failure morphology;
7. compare within nominally similar pairs/groups.

Interpretation:

- if incorporating the observed waveform removes the load discrepancy without changing the material model, the previous error was substantially structural/imperfection-induced;
- if the observed waveform is correctly represented but a systematic bias remains, that residual is evidence against the current material constraint/current operator or against some other still-frozen physical input;
- if specimens with similar material but different observed waveform produce different residual patterns, the 2D material constraint should be checked against the associated stress-path/domain differences rather than globally retuned.
