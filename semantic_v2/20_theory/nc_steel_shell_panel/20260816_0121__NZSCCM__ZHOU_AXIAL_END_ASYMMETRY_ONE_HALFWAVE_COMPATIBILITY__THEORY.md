## 2. Why fixing an entire bottom edge is not automatically a rigid-body gauge

An axial rigid translation has the form

\[
v(x,y)\mapsto v(x,y)+C,
\]

with one spatial constant `C`.

Therefore a complete-edge condition `v(x,0)=0` is a pure datum **only if** the unconstrained bottom trace differs from zero by one constant. Any x-dependent trace component cannot be removed by a rigid translation or by the scalar mean-shortening coordinate `D`.

This distinction is functional, not numerical.

## 4. Exact admissible axial trace mode

Consider the full-panel axial displacement variation

\[
\boxed{
 v_A=\frac{b}{\pi}\eta\cos2X\,(1-\cos Z)
}
\]

with no accompanying transverse displacement variation.

It satisfies the Zhou essential bottom condition exactly:

\[
v_A(x,0)=0.
\]

At the physical top,

\[
v_A(x,a)=\frac{2b}{\pi}\eta\cos2X,
\]

which is admissible because top `uy` is not an essential displacement condition.

The top trace has zero width mean,

\[
\int_0^\pi \cos2X\,dX=0,
\]

so it is neither a rigid axial translation nor a change of the scalar mean-shortening coordinate `D`. For any x-uniform axial resultant/traction, its external generalized work also vanishes by the same zero-mean identity.

For `a=2b`, the normalized membrane strains generated by unit `eta` are

\[
B_A:\quad e_x=0,
\]

\[

\]

\[
 \gamma_{xy}=-2\sin2X(1-\cos Z).
\]

The lateral in-plane edges have no essential displacement conditions, so this is a conforming Ritz variation; lateral traction conditions remain natural conditions of stationarity.

## 5. Exact coefficient-space generalized forcing

Use the isotropic plane-stress elastic bilinear form on the full normalized panel,

\[
\langle e,f\rangle_\nu=
\int_0^\pi\int_0^\pi
\left[
 e_xf_x+e_yf_y+\nu(e_xf_y+e_yf_x)
 +\frac{1-\nu}{2}\gamma_e\gamma_f
\right]dX\,dZ.
\]

All factors are finite integer trigonometric polynomials in `X,Z`, so every moment is evaluated analytically. Direct product-to-sum integration gives

\[
\boxed{
f_A^G=\langle B_A,G\rangle_\nu=\frac{\pi}{120}\neq0
}
\]

for every `nu`.

The self stiffness is

\[
\boxed{
K_A=\langle B_A,B_A\rangle_\nu
=\frac{\pi^2}{16}(25-24\nu)>0
}
\]

for the physical range of Poisson ratio.

For the uniform axial shortening source `D=(0,-1,0)`,

\[
\boxed{
f_A^D=\langle B_A,D\rangle_\nu=0.}
\]

Thus this x-dependent axial-end trace mode is not a mean-load coordinate, yet the m=2 FvK source projects onto it directly.

If this mode were isolated for a unit geometric driver, its stationary coefficient would be

\[
\eta_G=-\frac{f_A^G}{K_A}
=-\frac{2}{15\pi(25-24\nu)}.
\]

## 6. Why the 01:02 symmetric one-halfwave axial family is incomplete for the full Zhou boundary

The 01:02 axial correction family on the lower physical half-panel was

\[
v_{rs}=\frac b\pi V_{rs}\cos(rX)\sin(sY),
\qquad Y=\pi y/\ell=2Z,
\]

with integer `s`.

Every such correction satisfies

\[
v_{rs}(X,0)=v_{rs}(X,\pi)=0.
\]

Therefore, when this family is used as a symmetric representative-halfwave reduction, it suppresses x-dependent axial correction trace at the physical midheight and, under repeated/symmetric reconstruction, at the opposite loaded end. The scalar `D` can restore only an x-uniform axial displacement difference.

The exact lower-half restriction of the admissible full-panel mode above is

\[
\boxed{
 v_A\big|_{0\le y\le a/2}
 =\frac b\pi\eta\cos2X\left[1-\cos\left(\frac Y2\right)\right].
}
\]

The factor `cos(Y/2)` is a half-integer harmonic with respect to the one-halfwave coordinate `Y`. It is not a finite member of the current integer-trigonometric D15 basis.

It can be represented only through an infinite integer-harmonic expansion or through a separate exact trace-condensation operator. Therefore the **current finite 01:02 symmetric family is not exactly equivalent to Zhou's full axial-end boundary class**.

This finding does not invalidate the transverse PF closure and does not revoke the project rule `ONE_CONTINUOUS_COMPLETE_HALFWAVE`. It means the full-panel axial-end asymmetry must be analytically condensed onto that single representative halfwave before nonlinear production.
