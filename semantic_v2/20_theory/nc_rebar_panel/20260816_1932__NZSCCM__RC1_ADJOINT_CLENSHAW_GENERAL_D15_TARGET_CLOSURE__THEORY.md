## 2. Adjoint Clenshaw identity

For

\[
f(g)=\sum_{k=0}^{N}c_kT_k(g)
\]

use the standard backward Clenshaw recursion

\[
b_k=c_k+2gb_{k+1}-b_{k+2},
\qquad b_{N+1}=b_{N+2}=0,
\]

\[
f=c_0+gb_1-b_2.
\]

Let `L_K[h]=D15[K h]` be an exact target functional. The transpose recursion starts from

\[
A_1=gK,\qquad A_2=-K
\]

and, for `k=1,...,N`, contributes

\[
c_k L[A_k]
\]

while propagating

\[
\boxed{A_{k+1}\leftarrow A_{k+1}+2gA_k}
\]

\[
\boxed{A_{k+2}\leftarrow A_{k+2}-A_k}.
\]

Together with `c0 L[K]`, this gives exactly

\[
\boxed{L_K[f(g)]}
\]

without first constructing the forward sequence `T_0(g),...,T_N(g)`.

Thus the transpose/adjoint Clenshaw algebra itself is valid.

\[
