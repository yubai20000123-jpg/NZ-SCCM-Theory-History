# BH100 steel-shell audit after UHPC damage-driver correction

Status: steel branch NOT accepted; smaller BH cases remain paused.

## Frozen comparison state
Use the corrected UHPC state at w = 82.15506222 mm:
- PUHPC = 7.21811 MN
- rhoA = 0.8533161
- rhoD = 0.7715349
- wP = 31.59680 mm

The previously reported steel forces were:
- TOP = 2.96108 MN -> mean axial stress 148.05383 MPa
- BOTTOM = 5.43196 MN -> mean axial stress 271.59798 MPa

## Audit 1: the large TOP/BOTTOM split is introduced mainly by the whole-face Mises corrector
At the same state, before the corrector the Yun mean-stress formula gives:
- TOP elastic predictor mean axial stress = 251.08787 MPa
- BOTTOM elastic predictor mean axial stress = 271.59798 MPa

Thus before the corrector the TOP/BOTTOM difference is only 20.51 MPa.

The TOP corrector changes the recoverable local amplitude from
A_plus = 4.4312342 mm
to
e_plus = 1.8956005 mm

and, because the same scalar e is inserted into the whole-face mean Yun formula, the TOP mean axial stress drops from 251.09 to 148.05 MPa. This is a 103.03 MPa whole-face mean loss, or about 41 percent, triggered by the maximum Mises condition at only the controlling part of the face.

The BOTTOM face has not yet activated that corrector, so it remains at 271.60 MPa. Hence almost all of the extreme TOP/BOTTOM split is generated by the global single-e corrector, not by the original whole-face geometry.

Combined steel force:
- before TOP corrector: about 10.454 MN
- after TOP corrector: about 8.393 MN
The single controlling Mises event therefore removes about 2.061 MN from the total two-face steel force at once.

## Audit 2: global geometry alone does NOT support the reported 148 vs 272 MPa split
For the overall field only, with z_f = +/-23 mm, the exact area-mean steel axial stress is

mean_sigma_y_g_plus/minus =
Es/(1-nus^2) * [
mean_eps_y0 + nus*mean_eps_x0
+ z_f_plus/minus*(beta^2+nus*alpha^2)*w*(4/pi^2)
].

At w = 82.15506222 mm:
- global-only TOP mean compression = 223.793 MPa
- global-only BOTTOM mean compression = 299.077 MPa

The mean overall axial strain is about 0.0012691; the top/bottom axial-strain deviation caused by overall bending is only about +/-5.95 percent of that mean. Therefore the overall shape is indeed a small-eccentricity / near-axial-compression state in an area-average sense.

The global field creates a finite top/bottom difference, but not the collapse to 148 MPa on the TOP face.

## Audit 3: TOP global field itself reaches local Mises yield before the reported total peak
Using only the overall steel field and checking both steel-thickness surfaces, the TOP outer surface reaches 355 MPa at approximately

w = 78.688 mm.

At w = 82.155 mm:
- TOP outer global-only max Mises ≈ 375.43 MPa
- BOTTOM global-only max Mises ≈ 337.57 MPa

Therefore, by the reported total peak, TOP already contains a localized global-yield region even before the Yun local increment is added.

The current implementation does not first correct this global steel state. Instead it leaves the global elastic field unchanged and reduces a single whole-face local recoverable amplitude e until the COMBINED maximum Mises returns to 355 MPa. In effect, local unloading is being used to rescue a globally over-yield elastic trial state. That is not a valid separation of global plasticity and local postbuckling.

A direct pointwise current-state Mises cap applied to the global field changes the area-mean TOP axial stress by less than about 1 MPa in an independent numerical checker, because the yielded area is localized. This confirms that local first yield should not cause an immediate 103 MPa collapse of the whole-face mean TOP stress.

## Root cause
The current steel branch conflates three different objects:
1. overall/global steel stress due to the common UCFT deformation;
2. local Yun postbuckling stress redistribution;
3. steel plastic limiting.

The single scalar recoverable amplitude e is being used to enforce the maximum Mises condition over the entire face and is then substituted back into the whole-face mean Yun stress. Thus one local yield point globally scales the face response.

This is the main source of the unrealistic TOP/BOTTOM force split.

## Corrected steel direction
Keep total local geometry A as the geometric variable. Do not reduce the entire face to a single recoverable e for force recovery.

For each continuous point of the steel face:
1. build the current global base stress;
2. correct global base locally if it exceeds the Mises surface;
3. add the Yun/R06 local elastic increment;
4. compute an explicit local remaining-capacity factor lambda_l in [0,1] from
   Phi(sigma_g + lambda_l * delta_sigma_l) = fy^2.
Because plane-stress Mises Phi is quadratic in stress, this is a quadratic equation in lambda_l at every point.
5. integrate the resulting continuous sigma_y field over the whole face to obtain the face average and axial force.

This makes yielded regions expand continuously. A single controlling point can no longer collapse the whole-face average stress.

No spatial Gauss/material-point discretization is required in the theory: the yield boundary is an algebraic level set and the face force remains a continuous integral.

## Secondary open issue
The current steel global strain interface is still driven by the elastic UHPC mother-field quantity NyE/(Ec tc). Since the final UHPC force is damage/plastic reduced, the consistency of this interface must be audited after the steel plasticity correction. It should not be changed simultaneously in the present steel A/B test.

## Decision
Do not accept the current 15.611-MN BH100 result as a final theory result.
Do not resume BH085-BH005.
Next executable target: BH100 only, replace the single-e whole-face corrector by the continuous local Mises-cap operator above, keeping the corrected UHPC damage activation frozen.
