## 1. Base 2x2 Cayley–Hamilton identity

\[
E^2-J_1E+J_2I=0,
\qquad J_1=\operatorname{tr}E,
\qquad J_2=\det E.
\]

If

\[
E^n=a_n(J_1,J_2)I+b_n(J_1,J_2)E,
\]

then

\[
a_{n+1}=-J_2b_n,
\qquad
b_{n+1}=a_n+J_1b_n,
\]

with `(a0,b0)=(1,0)`, `(a1,b1)=(0,1)`.

Therefore every finite polynomial scalar principal law

\[

\]

has the exact matrix lift

\[
\boxed{f(E)=A_f(J_1,J_2)I+B_f(J_1,J_2)E}
\]

with finite polynomial `A_f,B_f`. No explicit principal-value square root remains.

This proves that the first spectral root

\[
\lambda_{1,2}=\frac{J_1\pm\sqrt{J_1^2-4J_2}}2

is **not by itself** the fundamental obstacle.
