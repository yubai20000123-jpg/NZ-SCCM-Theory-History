4.3 FINITE ELEMENT FORMULATION FOR THE OUT-OF-

PLANE STABILITY OF REINFORCED CONCRETE WALLS



There are two methodologies in the finite eJement analysis of reinforced concrete

structures. The first is to use discrete elements for the reinforcement and concrete,

with the steel being modelled using beam elements and the concrete being modelled

using plate bending elements (Wanchoo et al. 1975, Foster 1992 etc.). In the

second methodology, the steel is uniformly smeared across the concrete to give a

single constitutive relationship (for example Iofriet and McNeice 1971).



Both methods have their disadvantages and advantages. Using discrete elements to

model concrete and steel provides more latitude in the location of reinforcement, for

example, in the case when the wall has openings. However, as the beam elements

can only be placed at the boundaries of the plate elements, more elements may be

required than for the smeared method. The smeared approach works well when the

reinforcement is placed uniformly along the orthogonal axes. For the case of walls

with openings or with concentrated in-plane loading (for exampJe, loads transferred

through columns to the wall) the smeared approach may give inadequate results (see

Foster 1992 for the analysis of reinforced concrete deep beams).



In this study the discrete element approach is used to model the concrete and

reinforcement. Concrete is modelled by 4-node, rectangular 16-degrees-of-freedom

eJements, whiJe the reinforcing steel is modelled by 2-node, 4-degrees-of-freedom

beam elements.
